# The myth of the hooded hacker

Films show a lone genius breaking into a bank by typing fast. Real attackers look nothing like this.

- Most attacks are **not** aimed at you personally: they are automated, sweeping the whole internet for any weak system.
- Many serious attackers are **organized groups**, run like businesses, not lone geniuses.
- Most break-ins use **ordinary weaknesses**: a reused password, an unpatched program, a convincing email.

Believing the myth leads to defending against the wrong attacks.

---

<!-- 1.3 -->

# Script kiddies and opportunists

- **Script kiddies** use tools others built, without deep understanding. The tools are powerful, so the damage is real.
- **Opportunists** aren't after you specifically; they take any easy target. Most automated attacks are opportunistic, scanning millions of addresses for a known weakness.

**Defense:** basic security stops most of them. Update software, use strong unique passwords, don't expose needless services.

**Key lesson:** you don't need to be unbreakable, only **harder than the next target**.

---

<!-- 1.4 -->

# Cybercriminals and ransomware crews

The largest serious threat to most businesses: **organized cybercrime**, run like an industry.

- **Ransomware-as-a-service:** some write the malware and rent it out; others break in; others launder money.
- **Double extortion:** they steal the data first, *then* lock it, so they can threaten to publish even if you have backups.
- **Motive:** money, directly. They pick targets by profit, not grudge.

**Defense:** defense in depth, **offline backups above all**, training, patching, MFA.

---

<!-- 1.5 -->
<!-- layout: recap -->

# Recap: the real attackers

- Real attacks are mostly automated, opportunistic, and use ordinary weaknesses.
- Basic hygiene stops most opportunists: be harder than the next target.
- Organized ransomware crews are the biggest money threat; offline backups are the key defense.

# Insiders

An **insider** already has legitimate access: an employee, contractor, or supplier. Dangerous because they're already inside your defenses.

- **Malicious:** abuse access on purpose, to steal, defraud, or out of anger.
- **Accidental:** cause harm by mistake, far more common, phishing, misconfiguration, a lost laptop.

**Defense:** least privilege, logging and monitoring, separation of duties, and a culture where people report mistakes instead of hiding them.

---

<!-- 2.3 -->

# Hacktivists

**Hacktivist** = hacker + activist. They attack to make a political or social point.

- **Aim:** attention, not money, defacing a site, leaking documents, knocking a service offline in protest.
- **Skill:** varies, basic to capable.
- **Targets:** organizations they oppose.

**Defense:** the fundamentals, plus extra care for public-facing systems and the ability to withstand a flood of traffic.

---

<!-- 2.4 -->

# Nation-states and APTs

Government-funded teams. Their long campaigns are called **APTs**: Advanced Persistent Threats.

- **Advanced:** well-funded, highly skilled, sometimes using unknown weaknesses (**zero-days**).
- **Persistent:** they don't give up; they may hide for months or years.
- **Threat:** real intent and capability.

**Motives:** espionage, sabotage, influence. **Defense:** you usually can't outspend a government, aim to be costly to hit, detect fast, and limit damage. Small firms are often hit as a **stepping-stone** to a bigger target.

---

<!-- 2.5 -->
<!-- layout: recap -->

# Recap: insiders to nation-states

- **Insiders** are trusted and already inside; accidents outnumber malice. Least privilege and logging help.
- **Hacktivists** want attention; protect public-facing systems.
- **Nation-states / APTs** are skilled and persistent; detect fast and limit damage.

# The basic motives

Knowing the motive predicts what an attacker wants and how they'll behave.

| Motive | What they want | Typical attacker |
|---|---|---|
| **Money** | Payment, fraud, resold data | Cybercriminals, ransomware |
| **Information** | Secrets, designs, personal data | Nation-states, competitors |
| **Disruption** | Stop a service, cause chaos | Hacktivists, insiders |
| **Belief** | Make a point, gain attention | Hacktivists |

Most business attacks are about **money**.

---

<!-- 3.3 -->
<!-- layout: table -->

# Know your threat model

Defend against the attackers you are actually likely to face.

| Organization | Likely attackers |
|---|---|
| Small shop | Opportunists, ransomware crews |
| Hospital or bank | The above + targeted criminals, fraud rings |
| Government / defense | The above + nation-states |
| **Everyone** | **Insiders** |

Most attacks are opportunistic and use ordinary weaknesses, so **ordinary defenses stop most of them**.

---

<!-- 3.4 -->
<!-- layout: recap -->

# Recap: motives and defense

- Four motives: money, information, disruption, belief. Most business attacks are money.
- Match your defenses to the attackers you'll really face; everyone faces insiders.
- You rarely need to be unbreakable, just harder than the next target, and quick to detect and recover.
