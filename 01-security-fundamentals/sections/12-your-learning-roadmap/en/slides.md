# How the program is organized

A path from knowing nothing to being ready for work, in five stages:

| Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Foundations** | The ground every security person stands on |
| **Offensive** | How attacks really work, hands-on |
| **Defensive** | How to watch, catch, and recover |
| **Engineering** | How to build systems that resist attack |
| **Specialized** | Deep fields, and turning skills into a job |

Each stage depends on the ones before. **Rule one: do the Foundations first, in order, and don't rush them.**

---

<!-- 1.3 -->

# The Foundations, and why they come first

"I want to hack things now" is the trap. Those who struggle later skipped the basics.

- **How Computers Work:** you can't analyze what you don't understand.
- **Linux:** the native home of security tools.
- **Networking:** the language of nearly every attack and defense.
- **Windows / AD:** what most organizations run, and attackers target.
- **Scripting / Python:** automate, and build your own tools.
- **Home Lab:** your safe, legal place to practise.
- **Cryptography:** the math behind passwords, HTTPS, signatures.

The alphabet and grammar before the essays.

---

<!-- 1.4 -->

# Choosing a path

After the Foundations, specialize, matching the role families:

- **Offensive:** you love figuring out how things break.
- **Defensive:** you like investigating, noticing patterns, staying calm. **Most jobs; a reliable way in.**
- **Engineering:** you like building and designing well.

You can broaden later, understanding more than one side makes you more valuable.

**Unsure? Start Defensive:** broadest job market, strong base to pivot from.

# Learn by doing: the home lab

**You cannot learn security by reading alone.** Reading about lock-picking doesn't make you a locksmith.

A **home lab** = virtual machines on your own computer: an attacker, vulnerable targets, an isolated network. Everything is yours, so the ethics rule is satisfied by design.

- **Do, don't just watch.** Perform every technique yourself.
- **Break things, then fix them.** Attack *and* defend.
- **Take notes** (they become your portfolio).
- **Reset and repeat** with snapshots.

---

<!-- 2.3 -->

# Practise: CTFs and challenges

Free, legal practice the community runs.

- **CTFs (Capture The Flag):** solve challenges to find a hidden flag, web, crypto, forensics, reversing, pwn.
- **Practice platforms:** deliberately vulnerable machines, legal because they're built for it.

Why: legal, graded (you know when you win), builds real stamina, connects you to a community.

**30 minutes a few times a week compounds into serious skill.**

---

<!-- 2.4 -->

# Build a portfolio

**Skills and a portfolio get you hired.** Start now, before your first job.

- **Write-ups:** how you solved a challenge, the weakness, the fix, skill *and* communication.
- **A blog or notes site:** teaching proves understanding.
- **Tools/scripts** you've written; **documented lab work.**

Keep it **honest** (integrity is everything) and **clear** (a well-explained simple project beats a vague complex one).

Start your write-up habit with your very next challenge.
