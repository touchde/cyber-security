## How to Use This Book

This book is the first course in a cyber security program that takes you from knowing nothing to being ready for professional work. It assumes no security, no programming, and no networking. It has 12 sections. Each section builds on the ones before it, so read them in order.

Every section follows the same plan:

- **What You Will Learn** and **Before You Start** say what the section covers, and what you should already know.
- **Numbered topics** teach one idea each, in plain language, with concrete examples, tables, and diagrams.
- **Common Mistakes** collects the errors people actually make, so you can avoid them.
- **Summary**, **Check Your Understanding**, and **Practice** help you remember the section. Every check-question has an answer, and every practice has a worked example.

**This is a thinking course, not a typing course.** Security Fundamentals builds the mental model and the language of the field. The hands-on, keyboard skills, Linux, networking, scripting, and attacking and defending in a lab, come in the courses that follow. There is almost no code here, and you need no special software to read this book.

**The one rule, from the first page.** Everything in this program is for defending. Later courses teach real attacks, but you only ever try them on systems you own, or that you have clear written permission to test, such as the home lab you will build. Attacking anyone else's computer, phone, website, or account is a crime in Cambodia and almost every country. Section 9 covers the ethics and the law in full.

**How the examples work.** The book uses small, realistic situations to explain ideas, a business hit by ransomware, a clinic's data, a shop's customer list. These are illustrations, not a running story: each section stands on its own, so if you already know a topic, you can skip to the next. Every name, key, password, and figure in an example is made up.

**Getting the most from it.** Read actively: at each **Practice**, stop and do it before reading the worked example. Keep notes as you go; those notes are the start of the portfolio that section 11 and section 12 explain. And when you finish, move straight on to the next Foundation course, in order, this book's final section is your roadmap for everything that follows.
